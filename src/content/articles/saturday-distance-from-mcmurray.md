The hardest part of a first Saturday is not the pace. It is not knowing when to turn around.

The lot at 212 Valley Brook Road in McMurray sits on an out-and-back. You run out. You turn. You finish at the same trail sign you left. That is the whole navigation system. If you decide the turnaround before you start, you cannot get lost in the distance.

This is how to pick that turnaround.

## The lot and the route we already measured

GRYT meets at 212 Valley Brook Road, McMurray, PA 15317, every Saturday at 7:00 AM. Parking is free. Look for the group near the trail sign. The run is on the Montour / Peters Trail, flat and shaded, out and back.

The route saved on the homepage is 4.2 miles with 81 feet of gain. On Strava it is named GRYT RUN CLUB (Valleybrook CC Bridge + Back). That is one good Saturday. It is not a requirement.

[Open that route on Strava](https://www.strava.com/routes/3538247257394386560)

The [FAQ](/faq) is plain about distance. You pick it. Turn around at 1 mile, at 2 miles, or go long. Most people do 3 to 6 miles. Some go 8 or more. If you can walk for 30 minutes, you can do a Saturday. Nobody is timing you.

## Out-and-back math

On an out-and-back, the turnaround is half the distance you want.

| If you want | Turn around at | What that usually feels like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 miles | 1 mile out | A first Saturday, or a walk-heavy morning |
| 3 miles | 1.5 miles out | The distance a lot of regulars settle into |
| 4 miles | 2 miles out | Close to the saved Saturday route |
| 4.2 miles | The saved route | Out to the bridge and back, 81 feet of gain |
| 6 miles | 3 miles out | A longer morning. Bring water. |

You do not need a watch to do this. Pick a landmark at the turn, tell the person next to you, and come back. If you are new, tell someone at sign-in and you will get paired. Come 10 minutes early.

Water matters once the run gets long. The FAQ says bring water for anything longer than 45 minutes. A 2-mile walk may not need a bottle. A 6-mile morning does.

## Why the turnaround is the point

A loop makes you finish what you started. An out-and-back lets you change your mind without quitting the group.

If your legs are done at a mile, you turn. The people who went farther are still on the same trail, coming back toward you. The run ends when the last person finishes. Faster people loop back. You are not left at a far corner of a park hoping someone notices.

That is why the first question at the lot should be "where are you turning?" and not "what pace are you?"

## If 4.2 miles is not enough

The saved route is not the end of the pavement.

[Peters Township](https://www.peterstownship.com/Facilities/Facility/Details/Arrowhead-Trail-7) describes the Arrowhead Trail as 3.5 miles of paved trail from Route 19 to Brush Run Road. [TrailLink](https://www.traillink.com/trail/montour-trail/) says the Montour becomes that paved Arrowhead section after it passes under US 19 / Washington Road, and that most of the longer Montour is crushed limestone, with paved pieces in Peters Township and Clairton.

So the Saturday lot is on the easy paved piece. If you want more than the bridge turnaround, stay on the pavement and keep a landmark. Do not assume the whole 63-mile Montour system is the same surface as the first miles out of our lot. It is not.

We have not measured every side path from the Valley Brook pin, so this guide does not invent mile markers past the saved 4.2-mile route. If you want a number you can trust, use the saved route or turn on a simple out-and-back split.

More on the wider trail network is in the [South Hills rail trails guide](/resources/articles/south-hills-rail-trails-running).

## If you only have 45 minutes

You can still come.

A 2-mile out-and-back is 1 mile out and 1 mile back. At a walk, that is a full morning without a hero effort. At an easy jog, it is shorter than the coffee. Either one counts. The FAQ does not have a minimum pace. It has a minimum of showing up and picking a turn.

If you have an hour, 3 miles is the honest middle. Turn at 1.5. You will still be back before the lot empties. If you have 75 minutes and you want the saved route, do the 4.2. That is the bridge and back, 81 feet, not a hill workout.

Do not add distance in the lot because someone else said they are going long. Their long run is not your assignment. Tell them your turnaround. If they want company on the way back, they can loop. That is already how the no-drop format works.

## If the lot looks full

The homepage says the gravel lot holds about 20 cars. Saturday mornings get tight. Come at 6:50, not 7:05. If you are bringing someone, leave the extra minute so you are not hunting a spot while they stand by the sign alone.

The meeting point does not move if the lot is busy. Trail sign. Free lot. 212 Valley Brook Road.

## A simple plan for this Saturday

1. RSVP if it is your first time, and sign the short waiver. It takes under a minute, and you only sign it once.
2. Get to the lot by 6:50 AM.
3. Tell someone your turnaround: 1 mile, 1.5, 2, or the bridge.

5. Coffee, water, and sometimes fruit are in the lot after. Stay 10 minutes or stay an hour.

## FAQs

**Do I have to run the 4.2-mile route?**
No. That is the route we saved. You can turn at 1 mile or 2 miles and still finish with the group.

**How do I know when I have gone a mile?**
Use a watch if you have one. If you do not, run with someone who does, or pick the saved route and turn when they turn. The point is a decision, not a perfect split.

**Can I walk the short version?**
Yes. Walk-run is welcome. If you can walk for 30 minutes, you can do a Saturday.

**Is the whole Montour Trail this flat and paved?**
No. The Saturday miles are the flat paved Peters section. TrailLink describes most of the 63-mile Montour system as crushed limestone, with paved sections in Peters Township and Clairton.
