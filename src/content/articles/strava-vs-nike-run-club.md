Once you decide to start tracking your runs, the choice usually comes down to two apps: Strava or Nike Run Club. Both are free to download, both are widely used, and both will log your miles. But they're built around different ideas of what a runner needs, and those differences are worth understanding before you commit to one.

Here's a straight comparison to help you pick the one that fits how you actually run.

## What Each App Is Built For

### Strava

Strava is a social fitness platform. It tracks your runs, but the real draw is the community layer built around that data. You follow other athletes, give and receive kudos, compete on segment leaderboards, and share activities publicly or with a smaller circle. It's designed for runners who want context: how their pace on a specific stretch compares to last month, or how their weekly mileage is trending.

The free version covers GPS tracking, activity history, and the social feed. According to [Strava's subscription page](https://www.strava.com/subscribe), a paid subscription unlocks features including routes, segment leaderboards, and advanced training analysis. In the US, [Strava lists the price](https://www.strava.com/pricing) at $11.99/month or $79.99/year, with a $139.99/year family plan and a $149.99/year Strava + Runna bundle that adds custom training plans.

### Nike Run Club

Nike Run Club leans toward guided training. Its standout feature is [Audio-Guided Runs](https://www.nike.com/nrc-app): workouts narrated by Nike coaches and athletes that talk you through the run in your headphones. Nike's [app store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nike-run-club-running-coach/id387771637) describes 300+ free Guided Runs plus training plans, including a 4-week Get Started plan and a 12-week marathon plan (plans are available in the US and a handful of other countries). Nike also posts [free downloadable plans](https://www.nike.com/running/5k-training-plan), like an 8-week 5K plan where each run pairs with a Guided Run.

There's no paid tier for these core features.

## Head-to-Head: The Features That Actually Matter

### GPS Tracking and Route Data

Both apps track distance, pace, elevation, and splits using your phone or a connected watch. GPS accuracy depends more on your device than the app.

Strava's segment feature lets you compare your time on specific stretches of road or trail against your own history, and subscribers get full leaderboards to compare with other runners. NRC tracks your runs and personal records but doesn't have segments.

### Training Plans and Coaching

For free coaching, NRC has the edge. The guided runs are useful, especially for newer runners or anyone building toward a specific race, and the audio coaching helps on solo miles.

On Strava, structured plans come through the paid Strava + Runna bundle. The free version doesn't include training plans.

### Social Features and Community

Strava is built around its social feed. You can follow friends and local runners, join [clubs](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402172-clubs-on-strava), and see club events. If social features are what you want, it's the stronger tool.

NRC has a lighter social layer: friends, challenges you can create and share, and in-run audio cheers from friends.

### Cost

NRC's core features are free. On Strava, routes, segment leaderboards, and advanced analysis require the $11.99/month or $79.99/year subscription. The free tier is still worth having for logging and social features.

## Which One Makes More Sense for Pittsburgh Runners?

Pittsburgh runners cover a lot of ground: rail trails like the Montour Trail, hilly neighborhood roads, and training for races like the 2027 DICK'S Sporting Goods Pittsburgh Marathon on May 2, 2027.

If you're training for a race and want structure, NRC's free guided plans are hard to argue with. You get coaching, a schedule, and audio support without spending a dollar.

If you run with other people, want to follow progress on specific local routes, or want to connect with local clubs, Strava makes more sense.

Plenty of runners use both: NRC for guided runs and plans, Strava for logging and community. Just be careful not to record the same run twice if you have both syncing to the same watch or health app.

## FAQs

**Is Strava or Nike Run Club better for beginners?**
Nike Run Club is often the easier starting point because the free guided runs and plans give you structure from day one. Strava's free tier is more of a tracking and social tool, without built-in coaching.

**Do I have to pay for Strava to get value from it?**
No. The free version tracks your runs, shows your activity history, and includes the social feed and clubs. The subscription adds routes, segment leaderboards, and advanced analysis, which matter more to runners who love data.

**Can I use both Strava and Nike Run Club at the same time?**
Yes. Many runners use NRC for guided training and Strava for logging and community. Check your sync settings so runs don't get recorded twice.

**Can I track Montour Trail runs on Strava?**
Yes. Like any route, your runs there get logged with GPS, and you can compare your times on segments against your own history. Full segment leaderboards require a subscription.

**Is Nike Run Club really free?**
Its core features, including guided runs and training plans (in the US and select countries), are free, according to Nike's app listing.

**What's the best running app for someone training for a Pittsburgh-area race?**
NRC's free plans are a good no-cost option. If you want an adaptive custom plan and don't mind paying, the Strava + Runna bundle is another route. Check our [races page](/races) for upcoming local events.

**Do I need a running app to join GRYT Run Club?**
No. GRYT has no requirements around apps, pace, or training plans. Track your runs however you like, or don't. The group meets Saturdays at 7:00 AM on the Montour/Peters Trail in McMurray. First-timers RSVP online and sign a short waiver.

Both apps are worth having on your phone. Pick the one that fits your current goal, use both if it makes sense, and let each do what it does best. And if you want company on Saturday miles, [RSVP for a GRYT run](/).
