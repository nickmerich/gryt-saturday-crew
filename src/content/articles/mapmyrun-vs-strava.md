MapMyRun vs Strava is a real choice, and the right answer depends on what actually keeps you running. Apps track what you did. They do not make you go.

Strava is the stronger fit for runners who want a social feed, segment leaderboards, and club communities to stay motivated. MapMyRun is a solid pick if you mainly want straightforward GPS tracking, route mapping, and shoe mileage tracking. Both can complement an in-person group, but Strava is the clearer fit for community-minded runners who want a social layer built into their app.

The real decision comes down to one question: do you want an app that keeps you socially connected to other runners, or one that simply logs your runs and helps you plan where to go? Those are different problems, and these two apps solve them differently.

## What Is Strava and Who Is It For?

Strava tracks running, cycling, and many other activities, and wraps them in a social feed, segment leaderboards, and clubs. It works like a fitness-specific social network: every run you post shows up in a feed, your friends give you kudos, and you can compare yourself against anyone who has run the same stretch of road.

Segments are central to Strava's appeal. A segment is a defined portion of a route, and Strava ranks the people who have run it. That leaderboard keeps a lot of people coming back. Full leaderboards, the route builder, and advanced analysis are part of the subscription, per [Strava's pricing page](https://www.strava.com/pricing). A Family plan is also listed at $139.99 per year.

Strava's clubs let groups of runners share a feed, post announcements and events, and see each other's activity. For runners who train alone but want some thread connecting them to others, that feature does real work. Run clubs are growing fast on the platform: Strava reported that running clubs grew 3.5x in 2025.

Strava suits runners who want social accountability and competitive context. If you want the full feature set, plan on paying for it.

## What Is MapMyRun and Who Is It For?

MapMyRun is a long-running GPS run tracker. It was built by MapMyFitness, which Under Armour owned for years. Under Armour sold MapMyFitness to Outside Interactive in a deal that closed on August 30, 2024, according to the [MapMyFitness developer site](https://developer.mapmyfitness.com/blog/), which also says the platform has more than 80 million registered users. You may still see "by Under Armour" in the app's branding.

The app covers the basics well: GPS tracking, a run log, route mapping, and a gear tracker for logging mileage on your shoes so you know when a pair is wearing out. If you rotate between two pairs, each one gets its own count. Under Armour's connected-footwear program has been retired, so check the app for current shoe tracking options rather than counting on automatic syncing.

The paid MVP plan adds features listed on the [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/map-my-run-gps-running-tracker/id291890420), including live tracking, training plans, heart rate zones, and audio coaching. The App Store shows several MVP price points, so check the app for what you'll actually pay.

MapMyRun fits runners who want straightforward tracking and route tools and don't care much about social competition.

## How Do the Social and Community Features Stack Up?

Strava's community tools are more developed than MapMyRun's. Strava has clubs, a social feed, kudos, and segment leaderboards. MapMyRun has some social features, but community isn't its focus.

Strava's clubs work like a shared feed for a specific group. Members' runs show up together, organizers can post events, and the club page shows weekly activity so everyone can see who is putting in work. That visibility creates low-level accountability without anyone coordinating schedules.

Segment leaderboards add a competitive layer. You run a familiar road, and Strava tells you where you rank. Some runners find that motivating. Others find it stressful. It depends on whether comparison helps you show up or makes you dread opening the app.

MapMyRun doesn't have segment leaderboards. If staying connected to a running community through an app is a priority, Strava is the clearer choice. Both apps estimate calories burned. Treat those numbers as estimates on any platform.

## Can You Transfer Runs from MapMyRun to Strava?

You can usually move workout history between the two apps, though it can take some manual steps.

1. In MapMyRun, open a completed workout and look for an export option. Fitness apps commonly export workouts as GPX or TCX files, but menus change with updates, so check the current app or website for the exact path.
2. Once you have the exported file, open Strava on the web and use the upload option to import it. Strava accepts common workout file formats.
3. Repeat for each workout you want to move. If you have years of history, check MapMyRun's account settings for a bulk data export.
4. After importing, review each activity in Strava to confirm the distance, pace, and map look right. Imported files sometimes show small differences.

Also check each app's connected-apps settings. Syncing options between services change over time, and an automatic connection may save you the manual work.

## FAQs

### Is MapMyRun still active and being updated?

Yes. MapMyRun is still available in the app stores. It's now owned by Outside Interactive, which bought MapMyFitness from Under Armour in 2024, according to the [MapMyFitness developer site](https://developer.mapmyfitness.com/blog/). Check the current version in your app store for the latest features.

### Is MapMyRun accurate for GPS distance tracking?

GPS accuracy varies by device, location, and conditions for any running app, including both of these. Tall buildings, tree cover, and weak signal can cause drift. Keeping your phone's location services fully enabled, letting GPS lock on before you start, and using a dedicated GPS watch can all help.

### Does Strava work without a paid subscription?

Yes. Strava's free tier includes GPS tracking, the social feed, kudos, and clubs. The subscription ($11.99 per month or $79.99 per year in the US, per [Strava's pricing page](https://www.strava.com/pricing)) adds full segment leaderboards, routes, and advanced analysis. For casual community runners, the free tier covers the basics.

### Can you use both Strava and MapMyRun at the same time?

You can, though running two GPS apps at once drains your phone battery faster. A more practical approach is to record in one app and import or sync the activity into the other. Many runners pick one as their primary and use the other only for a specific feature.

### Which app is better for beginner runners?

Strava's social feed gives beginners outside accountability, which helps while the habit is still forming. Seeing other people's runs and getting kudos after your own is a small but real incentive to go again. MapMyRun's training plans and audio coaching (with MVP) can help if you want more structure. Either works. Whether you have people to run with matters more than which app you use to log it.

## Conclusion

Strava is the better fit if social connection and competitive motivation keep you consistent. MapMyRun is a good fit if you want simple tracking, routes, and training tools without a social feed. Both offer free tiers, and both put their best extras behind a subscription.

Start by deciding what actually makes you lace up: the leaderboard, the route, or the people waiting at the trailhead. If it's the people, no app solves that as well as showing up somewhere with a consistent crew.
